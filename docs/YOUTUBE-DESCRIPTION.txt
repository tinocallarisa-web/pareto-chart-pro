MAIN DESCRIPTION
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Most Power BI Pareto charts are built with a sorted column chart, a RANKX measure and a
cumulative DAX calculation — and they fall apart the moment you have more than a few
hundred categories. This walkthrough shows a different approach: two fields, no DAX, and a
chart that stays readable whether you have 400 customers or 500,000.

We use a real question — half a million customers, twelve account managers, which customers
actually matter — and answer it end to end.

WHAT YOU'LL LEARN
• Why the 80/20 rule is a heuristic, not a law — and how to find out what your business
  actually is (some are 90/10, some are 60/40)
• How to read a Pareto whose X axis is a share of entities rather than a list of names
• How to mark the exact point where cumulative value crosses your threshold
• Why concentration is a property of the segment, not the business — and what happens to
  the curve when you slice by channel
• How to go from the shape of the distribution to the actual list of accounts
• How the chart behaves for keyboard and screen-reader users

ABOUT THE VISUAL
Pareto Chart Pro ranks the entities you bind, groups them into bins of a fixed percentage
of the entity population, and overlays a cumulative percentage line with up to three
reference thresholds. Because the axis is a share of entities rather than one bar per
category, it stays readable at any size. It is certified by Microsoft, which means it makes
no external network calls of any kind — all calculation happens locally in Power BI.

In Power BI Service the visual streams data in segments and has been tested at 500,000
entities with slicers re-ranking correctly. In Power BI Desktop, segment streaming is a
platform limitation and the visual reads up to 30,000 rows.

FREE VS PRO
Everything in this video works in the free tier except one control: bin size. Free is fixed
at 20% (five bars); Pro allows 1–20%, up to 100 bars. Bin size is also what determines how
many entities sit behind a bar, which matters for cross-filtering large models — the
documentation has the measured figures.

FOLLOW ALONG
The dataset used here is synthetic and reproducible — 500,000 customers across four
channels, generated from a seeded script. Link in the documentation.
