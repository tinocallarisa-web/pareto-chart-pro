What Is a Pareto Chart — and How to Use One in Power BI

80/20 Analysis · ABC Classification · Free on AppSource

If you've ever been told to "focus on what matters most," a Pareto chart is the analytical tool that shows you exactly which 20% of inputs drives 80% of your results — without any manual sorting or complex formulas.

What Is a Pareto Chart?

A Pareto chart visualizes concentration: it ranks contributors from the most impactful to the least, and overlays a cumulative line that shows how quickly the total accumulates as you move down the list.

It's named after the Italian economist Vilfredo Pareto, who observed in the 1800s that roughly 80% of Italy's land was owned by 20% of the population. That same 80/20 pattern appears across business and nature:

  • 80% of revenue comes from 20% of customers
  • 80% of inventory movement is driven by 20% of SKUs
  • 80% of defects originate from 20% of root causes
  • 80% of support tickets come from 20% of product issues

The exact ratios vary — it's not always precisely 80/20 — but the pattern of concentration appears everywhere. A Pareto chart makes that concentration visible and measurable.

Anatomy of a Pareto Chart

A Pareto chart has three visual components working together:

① Sorted bars — entities ranked from the highest contributor on the left to the lowest on the right. Each bar shows that entity's share of the total.

② Cumulative line — a line that shows the running total as a percentage of the whole. It starts low on the left and climbs toward 100% as you move right.

③ Reference line — a horizontal marker (usually at 80%) with a vertical crosshair showing exactly which group of entities reaches that threshold. Everything to the left of the crosshair is your critical group.

Where Pareto Charts Are Used

Pareto analysis is a standard tool across multiple domains wherever prioritization based on impact matters:

  • Sales & revenue — Which customers to treat as key accounts? Which ones drive the majority of your revenue?
  • ABC inventory — Group products into A (top 70%), B (70–90%), C (90–100%) for differentiated stock policies.
  • Quality / Lean / Six Sigma — Which defect categories to tackle first? Fix the vital few, eliminate most of the defects.
  • Procurement — How concentrated is supplier spend? Visible supply chain risk at a glance.
  • Product portfolio — Which SKUs are long-tail items consuming resources for negligible volume?

Key Features

Automatic ranking & binning — entities are always sorted highest to lowest. By default each bar represents 10% of entities (10 bars total). With Pro you can set bin size from 1% to 20% — from a 100-bar granular view to a 5-bar executive summary.

Up to 3 reference lines — Line 1 is on at 80% by default. Change it to any threshold, add a second line, and a third Pro for ABC-style three-zone analysis. Each line has its own color and label.

Drilldown on hierarchies — add Category → Subcategory → SKU to the Entity field and the Pareto recalculates automatically at each drill level. No separate charts needed per level.

Cross-filtering — click a bin to filter all other visuals on the page. Pareto Chart Pro participates fully in Power BI's standard cross-filter model.

Outlier exclusion Pro — exclude the top or bottom N% of entities before binning. Useful when one dominant account distorts the distribution shape.

Value labels on bars Pro — show the % contribution above each bar. Essential for executive dashboards and exported screenshots.

Conditional formatting — apply standard Power BI color rules on bars via the fx button. Color by value range, percentile tier, or gradient.

High contrast & accessibility — automatically adapts to Windows/Power BI high contrast mode.

1

Install from AppSource

In Power BI Desktop → Visualizations pane → three dots → Get more visuals → search Pareto Chart Pro → Add. The icon appears in your pane immediately.

2

Bind your data

Drag a category field (Customer, SKU, Supplier, Defect Category) into Entity. Drag a numeric measure (Revenue, Units, Defect Count) into Value. The chart renders automatically.

3

Read the result

The bin where the cumulative line crosses the 80% reference line is your breakpoint. The entities to the left are your critical group — the 20% that drives 80% of the total.
